Changelog

What changed in each version, newest first.

Mac 1.0.1

15 November 2026

  • The first capture after a macOS update or a new install no longer shows “scanning…” for about a minute: Snapline gets text recognition ready in the background when it starts.
  • The buttons on the capture toolbars show the pointing hand.
  • Clicking the menu bar icon opens the menu. To take a screenshot with a click instead, change “Clicking the menu bar icon” in Preferences → General.
  • Google Drive uploads go to your existing Snapline folder after you sign in again, instead of a new folder each time.
  • The Choose… panels in Preferences open in front of Preferences, not behind it.
  • Installing with macOS/scripts/make-app.sh --install moves the app into Applications instead of copying it, so Spotlight and Launchpad no longer list Snapline twice.

Windows 0.1.0

15 November 2026

The first version for Windows 10 (version 1809) and 11, 64-bit. Download it.

  • Lives in the tray. Take a screenshot with PrtScn, CtrlShift1 or the tray menu.
  • Draw on it, then copy, save, print, edit, share, upload to your S3 bucket or Google Drive, or search for similar images.
  • Auto-hide and Copy Text use Windows' own text recognition and face detection, on your PC.
  • Installs for your user only, with no admin rights. The installer is not signed yet, so SmartScreen asks first.

Linux 0.1.0

15 November 2026

The first version for Linux desktops with X11 or Wayland (GNOME, KDE Plasma, Xfce and others), 64-bit Intel/AMD or ARM. Download it.

  • The same features as on Windows, as a .deb, an AppImage or a Flatpak.
  • Auto-hide and Copy Text use Tesseract, built into Snapline. Faces are not hidden yet.
  • On Wayland the desktop takes the screenshot and owns the shortcut; GNOME may ask once to allow it.
  • The tray icon needs a panel that shows tray icons; on GNOME, the AppIndicator extension.

Mac 1.0.0

28 October 2026

The first version, for Macs with Apple silicon and macOS 15 or newer.

  • Lives in the menu bar. Take a screenshot with ⇧⌘1, drag over an area, draw on it, then copy, save, print, edit, email, upload to your S3 bucket or Google Drive, or search for similar images.
  • Auto-hide covers card numbers, keys, passwords, phone numbers, emails and faces, read on your Mac.
  • Copy Text copies the text out of a screenshot.
